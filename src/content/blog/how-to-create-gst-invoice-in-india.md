## What is a GST Invoice?

A GST invoice (also known as a tax invoice) is a document issued by a registered seller to a buyer. It details the goods or services provided, the total amount due, and the specific tax amounts (CGST, SGST, and IGST) levied on those goods or services. Under the GST regime, issuing a tax invoice is mandatory for registered businesses when supplying taxable goods or services.

### Why is it Important?

1. **Legal Compliance**: The GST Act mandates that a registered person supplying taxable goods or services must issue a tax invoice.
2. **Input Tax Credit (ITC)**: Your buyers can only claim ITC if they possess a valid tax invoice issued by you.
3. **Record Keeping**: Invoices act as primary evidence of transactions and are essential during audits and return filings.

## Mandatory Fields in a GST Invoice

To be fully compliant with Indian GST laws, your tax invoice must contain the following fields:

### 1. Invoice Number and Date
- **Invoice Number**: A unique consecutive serial number, containing alphabets, numerals, or special characters (like hyphen or dash), not exceeding 16 characters. It must be unique for a financial year.
- **Date of Issue**: The exact date the invoice is generated.

### 2. Business Details (Supplier)
- **Name and Address**: Your registered business name and complete address.
- **GSTIN**: Your 15-digit Goods and Services Tax Identification Number.

### 3. Customer Details (Recipient)
- **Name and Address**: The buyer's business name and address.
- **Customer GSTIN**: If the buyer is registered, their GSTIN is mandatory. (If unregistered, the invoice is considered a B2C invoice).
- **Place of Supply**: The state name and state code, crucial for determining whether to charge IGST or CGST+SGST.

### 4. Item Details
- **Description**: Clear description of the goods or services.
- **HSN / SAC Code**: Harmonized System of Nomenclature code for goods, or Servicing Accounting Code for services. (Mandatory based on turnover limits).
- **Quantity and Unit**: How much of the product/service was supplied (e.g., meters, kg, pieces).
- **Rate and Total Value**: The base price of the item before taxes and discounts.

### 5. Tax Breakdown
- **Taxable Value**: The amount on which tax is calculated (after applying discounts).
- **Tax Rate**: The applicable GST slab (e.g., 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%).
- **Tax Amounts**: Clearly separated into Central GST (CGST), State GST (SGST), and Integrated GST (IGST). 

### 6. Signature
- A physical or digital signature of the authorized person representing your business.

## Types of GST Taxes (CGST, SGST, IGST)

Understanding when to apply which tax is the most common hurdle for new business owners.

- **Intra-state Supply (Same State)**: If you and your buyer are in the same state (e.g., both in Maharashtra), you must split the GST equally into **CGST** (Central) and **SGST** (State). For an 18% item, charge 9% CGST and 9% SGST.
- **Inter-state Supply (Different States)**: If you are in Maharashtra and your buyer is in Delhi, you charge a single **IGST** (Integrated) at the full 18%.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Incorrect GSTIN**: Always verify the client's GSTIN. A single typo can result in them losing their Input Tax Credit, leading to disputes.
2. **Missing Place of Supply**: Essential for inter-state transactions to determine tax allocation.
3. **Improper Invoice Sequencing**: Your invoice numbers must be sequential. Do not use random numbers or skip numbers within a financial year.
4. **Forgetting the HSN Code**: Make sure you check the latest CBIC guidelines regarding mandatory HSN digits based on your aggregate turnover.
