# GST Invoice Format Guide

When the Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented in India, it brought a unified tax structure but also strict compliance rules. One of the most common areas of confusion for business owners is the **GST Invoice Format**. 

Unlike a simple proforma or a standard bill of supply, a GST tax invoice must contain specific information to be considered legally valid by the government. In this guide, we’ll explore the mandated fields, the rules for formatting, and how to guarantee compliance.

## Does the Government Dictate a Specific Visual Layout?

**No.** The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) does not mandate a specific visual layout or design for your invoice. You are free to design it however you like—add your logo, use your brand colors, arrange the columns as you see fit. 

However, the CBIC *does* mandate **what data must be present** on the document. As long as all mandatory data fields are included, your visual format is valid.

## Mandatory Data Fields for a GST Invoice

Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, a tax invoice must contain the following particulars:

1. **Supplier Details**: Name, address, and GSTIN of the supplier.
2. **Consecutive Serial Number**: A unique number containing alphabets, numerals, or special characters. It cannot exceed 16 characters and must be unique for a financial year.
3. **Date of Issue**: The date the invoice was generated.
4. **Recipient Details (Registered)**: Name, address, and GSTIN or UIN, if registered.
5. **Recipient Details (Unregistered)**: Name and address of the recipient and the address of delivery, along with the name of the State and its code (if the taxable value is ₹50,000 or more).
6. **HSN Code or SAC**: Harmonized System of Nomenclature code for goods or Service Accounting Code for services.
7. **Description of Goods/Services**.
8. **Quantity and Unit** (e.g., metre, kg).
9. **Total Value** of supply of goods or services.
10. **Taxable Value** of the supply considering discount or abatement.
11. **Rate of Tax** (Central tax, State tax, Integrated tax, Union territory tax or cess).
12. **Amount of Tax** charged in respect of taxable goods or services.
13. **Place of Supply** along with the name of the State, in the case of a supply in the course of inter-State trade or commerce.
14. **Address of Delivery** where the same is different from the place of supply.
15. **Whether the tax is payable on reverse charge basis**.
16. **Signature** or digital signature of the supplier or their authorized representative.

## The Importance of HSN and SAC Codes

The HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) code is an internationally accepted coding system used to classify goods. SAC (Service Accounting Code) is used for services. 

The government uses these codes to determine the applicable tax rate. Recent changes have made mentioning the HSN code mandatory based on your previous year's aggregate turnover:
- Turnover > ₹5 Crores: 6-digit HSN code is mandatory for all B2B and B2C invoices.
- Turnover < ₹5 Crores: 4-digit HSN code is mandatory for B2B invoices.

## Time Limit for Issuing a GST Invoice

You cannot issue an invoice whenever you feel like it. There are strict timelines:
- **For Goods**: The invoice must be issued on or before the removal of goods for supply to the recipient.
- **For Services**: The invoice must be issued within 30 days of the supply of service. (45 days for banks and financial institutions).
