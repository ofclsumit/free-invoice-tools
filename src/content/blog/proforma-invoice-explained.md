# What is a Proforma Invoice? Explained with Examples

If you deal with international trade, customs, or large B2B corporate clients, you have likely been asked to send a **Proforma Invoice**. For many new business owners, this sounds like just a fancy word for a regular invoice. However, treating a proforma invoice like a standard tax invoice can lead to accounting nightmares and tax liabilities.

In this guide, we will explain exactly what a proforma invoice is, when to use it, and how to create one.

## What is a Proforma Invoice?

A **Proforma Invoice** is a preliminary bill of sale sent to a buyer *in advance* of a shipment or delivery of goods/services. "Pro forma" is Latin for "as a matter of form" or "for the sake of form." 

It outlines the exact details of the transaction: what is being sold, the price, the weight, the shipping costs, and the terms of sale. It looks exactly like a final invoice, but it is **not a demand for payment** and it is **not a true accounting document**.

### The Main Purpose
The primary purpose of a proforma invoice is to give the buyer a precise, confirmed cost so they can arrange for payment, get internal purchase approvals, or arrange for import/customs clearance.

## Proforma Invoice vs. Quotation

You might be thinking: *"Isn't this just a quotation?"*
They are very similar, but they occur at different stages:
- A **Quotation** is an offer. It is subject to negotiation. You send a quote when a client says, "How much would X cost?"
- A **Proforma Invoice** is sent *after* the client has agreed to the quote, but *before* you ship the goods. It is a commitment that the goods/services will be delivered at the exact prices listed.

## Proforma Invoice vs. Tax Invoice

This is the most critical distinction for your accounting:

| Feature | Proforma Invoice | Tax Invoice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Issued before delivery | Issued after delivery |
| **Legal Status** | Not legally binding | Legally binding |
| **Accounting** | Do not record in accounts | Must be recorded as revenue / accounts receivable |
| **Tax (GST)** | Cannot be used to claim ITC | Used by buyer to claim ITC |

*Warning: If you enter a proforma invoice into your accounting software as a standard invoice, you will artificially inflate your revenue and potentially pay taxes on money you haven't received yet.*

## When Should You Use a Proforma Invoice?

1. **Customs and Imports**: When shipping goods internationally, customs requires a proforma invoice to declare the value of the goods to calculate duties and taxes before the goods even arrive.
2. **Advance Payments**: If you require a 50% deposit before starting a massive project, a corporate client might need a proforma invoice to get the funds released by their accounting department.
3. **Internal Approvals**: Large corporations often need a proforma to generate an internal Purchase Order (PO).
