# Invoice vs Quotation: What is the Difference?

In the world of business, specific terminology matters. Two of the most commonly confused documents for new freelancers and small business owners are the **Invoice** and the **Quotation** (or quote). While they might look similar visually—both containing a list of items, prices, and totals—they serve entirely different purposes in the sales lifecycle.

Understanding the difference between an invoice and a quotation is critical for maintaining healthy cash flow, legal protection, and professional client relationships.

## What is a Quotation?

A **Quotation** (often simply called a quote) is a formal document given by a seller to a potential buyer, detailing the estimated cost of goods or services *before* the work begins or the order is finalized.

Think of it as a proposal. You are telling the client: *"If you choose to hire me or buy my products, this is exactly what I will provide, and this is exactly what it will cost."*

### Key Characteristics of a Quotation:
- **Timing**: Sent *before* the sale or service takes place.
- **Purpose**: To provide pricing so the client can make a decision or compare vendors.
- **Legality**: It is an offer. It is not legally binding until the client formally accepts it (usually via a signature or an email confirmation).
- **Flexibility**: Prices on a quotation can be negotiated. You might send multiple revisions of a quote before the client agrees.

## What is an Invoice?

An **Invoice**, on the other hand, is a commercial document issued by a seller to a buyer *after* the goods have been delivered or the services have been rendered (or in the case of advance payments, when the payment is strictly due). 

An invoice is a formal request for payment. It tells the client: *"I have provided the goods/services we agreed upon; you now owe me this specific amount of money."*

### Key Characteristics of an Invoice:
- **Timing**: Sent *after* the order is confirmed, goods are delivered, or services are completed.
- **Purpose**: To request payment and act as a legal record of the sale.
- **Legality**: It is a legally binding document that proves debt. It is used for accounting, tax reporting (like GST), and audits.
- **Rigidity**: Prices on an invoice are final. They reflect the agreed-upon terms (often stemming from the accepted quotation).

## The Workflow: From Quote to Cash

To visualize how they differ, let's look at the standard B2B (Business-to-Business) workflow:

1. **Inquiry**: A client asks for the price of redesigning their website.
2. **Quotation**: You send a **Quotation** detailing the scope of work (design, coding, hosting) and estimating the cost at ₹50,000.
3. **Acceptance**: The client reviews the quote, agrees to the price, and signs it.
4. **Execution**: You build and launch the website.
5. **Invoice**: You send an **Invoice** for ₹50,000, requesting payment within 15 days.
6. **Payment**: The client transfers the money.
7. **Receipt**: You optionally send a **Receipt** acknowledging the payment was received.

## Can a Quote and Invoice be the Same Document?

No. While they share structural similarities (itemized lists, totals, business addresses), you should never use them interchangeably. 

An invoice creates a tax liability. If you send an invoice instead of a quote, your accounting software (and potentially the tax authorities) will register it as revenue, even if the client hasn't agreed to the work yet. Always label your documents clearly as "QUOTATION" or "TAX INVOICE" at the very top.
