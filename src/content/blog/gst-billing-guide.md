# GST Billing Guide for Small Businesses in India

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) transformed the Indian taxation system, replacing a complex web of VAT, Service Tax, and Excise duties with a unified structure. While it simplified the national market, GST brought rigorous compliance requirements, especially regarding billing and invoicing.

For a new freelancer or small business owner, navigating these rules can feel overwhelming. This **GST Billing Guide** breaks down everything you need to know to stay compliant and bill your clients with confidence.

## Who Needs to Issue a GST Invoice?

Not every business in India needs to worry about GST. You only need to issue a GST Tax Invoice if you are a **registered taxable person** supplying taxable goods or services.

### Turnover Thresholds for Registration:
- **Service Providers**: Mandatory registration if aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 Lakhs (₹10 Lakhs in Special Category States).
- **Goods Suppliers**: Mandatory registration if aggregate turnover exceeds ₹40 Lakhs (₹20 Lakhs in Special Category States).
- *Note: If you sell inter-state (e.g., from Karnataka to Kerala) or via e-commerce platforms, registration is mandatory regardless of turnover.*

If you are not registered, you simply issue a commercial invoice without charging any tax.

## Tax Invoice vs. Bill of Supply

It is crucial to understand which document to issue:

1. **Tax Invoice**: Issued when a registered dealer supplies *taxable* goods or services. It shows the exact amount of GST charged, allowing the buyer to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC).
2. **Bill of Supply**: Issued when a registered dealer supplies *exempted* goods/services, or if the dealer is registered under the **Composition Scheme**. A Bill of Supply does not contain any tax amounts, and the buyer cannot claim ITC.

## The Three Components of GST

When creating your invoice, you must categorize the tax correctly based on the location of the supplier (you) and the place of supply (the buyer).

- **CGST (Central GST)**: Revenue collected by the Central Government for intra-state sales.
- **SGST (State GST)**: Revenue collected by the State Government for intra-state sales.
- **IGST (Integrated GST)**: Revenue collected by the Central Government for inter-state sales.

**Example Scenario:**
You are a web designer in Delhi charging ₹10,000 for a website (18% GST slab).
- If your client is also in Delhi, you charge 9% CGST (₹900) and 9% SGST (₹900). Total Invoice: ₹11,800.
- If your client is in Mumbai, you charge a flat 18% IGST (₹1,800). Total Invoice: ₹11,800.

## Dealing with B2B vs. B2C Invoicing

The rules for your invoice format change slightly depending on who you are selling to.

**B2B (Business to Business)**:
When selling to another registered business, you **must** include their GSTIN on the invoice. This is critical because their ability to claim ITC depends entirely on your invoice matching their GST returns. 

**B2C (Business to Consumer)**:
When selling to an end-consumer or an unregistered business, they do not have a GSTIN. 
- If the value is > ₹50,000 and it's an inter-state supply, you must explicitly mention the name of the State and its state code on the invoice.
